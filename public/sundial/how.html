How Sundial works

Sundial decides what to sell: which calls and puts, how many, on which expiry and at which strike. You decide what to buy. Sundial never places an order; it shows the plan and you trade it.

Rules agreed with Nik on 25 to 27 Nov 2026. Every rule here was tested on real or model prices first.

A day with Sundial

All day
The To sell column is the plan, worked out again every minute from the live price. The line under it says when: “Updated 2:14 PM ($150.37)”.
Every minute
Watches every name for a big drop (see Puts). If one comes, you get a push straight away.
3:45 PM
The plan locks, 15 minutes before the close (12:45 PM on a half day). You get one push for each name with something to sell. After that the card shows the locked plan, less what you have already sold.
As you sell
What you sell today on the plan’s expiry stays on the card, marked Done. Anything still to sell is New.

Calls

How many: the test

The most calls that keep the whole book from losing money if the stock rises anywhere from 0 up to its test size (+20% for NVDA), at any close in the next 5 trading days.

  • The whole book is shares, calls bought (LEAPs too), puts bought and everything sold.
  • Sold calls and puts count in full: their premium is the cushion.
  • Bought calls and puts count only what the rise does to them, not their slow daily decay.
  • Past the test size, a loss is accepted.
  • NVDA and SPCX: no single expiry takes more than half of the calls the test would allow with none sold.

So when the price rises after a sale, a book can end up over its test. Sundial then suggests nothing more on that name until there is room again.

After the sale

NVDA and SPCX

Early profit

If a call is worth half or less of what it sold for at 3:45 PM on its first or second trading day, buy it back. It shows in the Close column.

Every name

Then hold

After day 2, hold to expiry, even at 90% kept. No rolls. Closing late pays the ask for very little risk, and holding never delays the next sale.

  • In the money before expiry: hold. Home flags it so you can decide.
  • In the money at expiry: buy back and sell again. Shares are let go only from lots held over a year.
  • Earnings: NVDA and SPCX sell no calls on an expiry that spans a report. The Friday names and TLT sell nothing that week.

Puts

Puts are how the share positions grow: being assigned is fine. They are always held to expiry, with no early profit.

Big drop

When a name falls 2 daily SD below yesterday’s close (about 9% for NVDA, about 1.5% for TLT), checked every minute, Sundial sells extra puts a week out at the put table’s strike, and you get a push.

  • NVDA and SPCX: up to the cap of 30 open.
  • TLT: 10 extra at 2 daily SD, 20 in all that day at 3 daily SD (about 2.3%), never more than the shares still wanted (5,000 less what is held, in contracts).

At expiry

In the money: take the shares if the strike is under your average cost, otherwise buy back and sell again.

The words on the board

To sell
The plan. New until you sell it, then Done.
Close
NVDA and SPCX calls worth half or less of their price on day 1 or 2: buy back at 3:45 PM.
Working
Everything sold and still open, with what is left to earn.
Expiring today
How far each is from its strike, and what a buy-back would cost.
Banked this week
What expired or was bought back this week, and what it kept.
SD
A normal week’s move: the stock’s IV over one week. A daily SD is the same for one day.
The week’s move
Today’s price against the close 5 sessions back, in SD. Holidays do not count as sessions.
Trend
Under the 50-day line is down; over both the 50- and 200-day is up; anything else is mid.
Rich or cheap
Cheap whenever the stock’s last 20 days moved more than its IV says. Otherwise rich when IV sits in the top 30% of its range over the past year, or at 1.3 times the real moves or more; cheap when it sits in the bottom 20% of that range.
Turns short
On Home, for NVDA and SPCX: how far the stock can rise before the book’s delta goes below zero.

Who checks Sundial

Three checks watch the engine. You only hear from them when something needs a look.

10 AM, 12 PM, 2 PM, 4 PM

The check

Rebuilds every book from IBKR’s trades a second way, and checks the board is not suggesting too many or too few calls and puts.

Once a day, at a random time

The inspection

Works every name out again with its own code: settings, reads, strikes, counts, credit, delta, and every stored close against the exchange’s official closes.

Right after the inspection

The review

Claude reads the rules, the board and both checks, and writes a line on each name: a rule not followed, numbers that do not add up, stale data, or a sale that looks risky today. Your own trades are never a rule broken.
